ORIGINAL SPIDER-VERSE STORY

📖 The Midnight Signal

Six chapters. One city. A signal that should not exist.

Before you begin...

New York had always been loud. Sirens, trains, people arguing on street corners, music coming from open windows. But lately, there had been another sound beneath it all: a strange electronic pulse that only a few people seemed able to hear.

You were not looking to become a hero. You were trying to understand why the city felt different, why the lights flickered whenever you walked past, and why a mysterious symbol kept appearing in places where nobody remembered painting it.

Chapter 1 of 6
CHAPTER ONE

THE CITY THAT NEVER SLEEPS

The first signal appeared at 11:47 p.m. on a Tuesday. You remembered the exact time because you had checked your phone while walking home, annoyed that the battery was nearly dead. The streets were damp from an earlier rain, and every traffic light painted the pavement in streaks of red, yellow and green.

Then every screen on the block went black. The advertisement above the bus stop disappeared. The corner shop's television switched off. Even the digital clock outside the pharmacy froze. For three seconds, the entire street seemed to hold its breath.

A blue symbol flashed across the screens: a circle crossed by three sharp lines, shaped almost like a spiderweb. A low pulse travelled through the air. You felt it in your chest before you heard it in your ears. Then the lights returned, the traffic moved again, and everyone carried on as if nothing had happened.

Everyone except you. Your phone had recorded something your eyes had barely caught. When you replayed the video, the symbol appeared clearly for one frame. Under it was a line of text: UNIVERSE CONNECTION: UNSTABLE.

You searched the words online. Nothing useful appeared. You tried different spellings, checked local news and watched the video again. Each time, the same symbol flashed. You were about to give up when your phone vibrated with a message from an unknown number.

It contained only seven words: “If you can see it, run.” Before you could reply, another pulse rolled across the city. Somewhere above the rooftops, something answered.

CHAPTER TWO

THE STRANGER ON THE ROOFTOP

You should have gone straight home. That was the sensible choice, and you knew it. Instead, you followed the sound of the signal through an alley and up a metal staircase attached to an old apartment building. Each step groaned under your shoes.

At the top, the city stretched out beneath you. The skyline glowed against the clouds, but a patch of darkness moved between two buildings where no shadow should have been. It looked like a hole cut into the night itself. Blue sparks flickered along its edges.

Someone stood near the rooftop ledge. They wore a dark suit with bright lines running down the arms and across the mask. Their face was hidden, but their posture suggested they had been waiting for you. One gloved hand held a small device that flashed in time with the strange signal.

“You heard it,” the stranger said. It was not a question. You asked who they were and why they had sent the message. They looked towards the dark opening in the sky.

“My name doesn't matter yet. What matters is that the signal found you before the thing on the other side did.” The stranger explained that the opening was a fracture between realities. Every pulse made it wider, and every time it widened, pieces of another world leaked into this one.

You asked why you could see it when everyone else seemed unaware. The stranger studied you for a moment. “Because something crossed over a long time ago. It left a mark on this universe. And somehow, that mark is connected to you.”

Before you could ask another question, the rooftop shook. A black shape slipped through the fracture and landed on the water tower. It had glowing eyes and moved like a corrupted video. The stranger raised their device. “Stay behind me.”

But the symbol on your phone began to shine through the screen. Your hands tingled. A thin blue thread shot from your wrist and attached itself to a chimney across the roof. You stared at it, unable to explain what you had just done.

The stranger slowly turned towards you. “Well,” they said, “that changes everything.”

CHAPTER THREE

THE POWER YOU DIDN'T ASK FOR

The creature lunged. You reacted without thinking. The blue thread tightened, pulling you sideways just before the creature struck the rooftop where you had been standing. Gravel scattered into the air. Your heart raced so hard that you could hear it over the wind.

“Move!” the stranger shouted. You tried to run, but your feet left the ground. For one impossible second, you were hanging above the roof with a web stretched between your wrist and the chimney. Then you swung forward, crashed into a ventilation unit and landed in a heap.

It was not graceful. It was not heroic. It hurt, and the stranger had to pull you upright before the creature noticed you again. Still, the web had appeared because you had needed it. That thought was almost more frightening than the monster.

The stranger introduced themselves as a guardian from another universe. They had been tracking the fracture for weeks, trying to stop it from swallowing pieces of different realities. The creature was called a Glitch: a living error that could distort anything it touched.

“Your abilities are reacting to the fracture,” the guardian explained. “That doesn't mean you understand them. And it definitely doesn't mean you're ready to fight something like that.”

You almost laughed at the idea of being ready. A few minutes earlier, you had been checking your phone and thinking about what to eat. Now you were on a rooftop with a stranger from another universe, a monster that could bend reality and a new ability that did not come with instructions.

The guardian handed you a small wrist device. It displayed a map of the city with several blinking points. “These are places where the signal is strongest. We need to investigate them before the fracture spreads. You need practice, and I need someone who can sense the signal without a machine.”

You could refuse. You could walk away and try to return to normal. But the creature had already crossed into your world, and pretending nothing was happening would not make it disappear.

“Teach me,” you said. The guardian nodded once. “Then lesson one: don't confuse having power with knowing how to use it.”

CHAPTER FOUR

TRAINING ABOVE THE CITY

Training began before sunrise. The guardian chose an abandoned construction site with high platforms, hanging beams and enough open space to practise without breaking every window nearby. You expected dramatic lessons about courage and responsibility. Instead, the first task was learning how to land without falling over.

The first hour was a disaster. Your webs attached to the wrong surfaces. You swung too far, stopped too late and once ended up hanging upside down from a sign. The guardian did not laugh, which somehow made the situation worse. They simply asked you to try again.

Slowly, you began to understand the rhythm. Aim first. Trust the line, but pay attention to where it leads. Keep your eyes open. Your powers responded to intention, but they still required practice. A strong web was useless if you sent it towards a surface that could not hold your weight.

Between exercises, the guardian showed you recordings of other universes. Some were bright and peaceful. Others had cities built beneath enormous glass domes, or trains that travelled through the sky. In one recording, the same blue symbol appeared on a wall. The image broke apart before the guardian could explain it.

“The signal is older than this fracture,” they admitted. “Someone or something has been trying to connect these worlds for a long time. I thought I was tracking the source. Now I think the source may be tracking us.”

That evening, you finally managed a clean swing from one platform to another. Your feet touched the ground, your knees bent, and you stayed upright. The guardian gave a small approving nod. It was not a grand celebration, but you found yourself smiling.

For the first time since the signal appeared, you felt less like a person caught in the middle of something impossible and more like someone who could make a choice. You still did not know why the powers had chosen you. You did know that you could learn.

Then the wrist device sounded an alarm. Every blinking point on the map turned red at once. Across the city, the fracture had begun opening in several places. The guardian looked at you. “Training is over. We have our first real mission.”

CHAPTER FIVE

THE NIGHT EVERYTHING CHANGED

The first fracture opened above the train station. The second appeared near the river. A third flickered over the shopping district, scattering strange blue reflections across the windows. The guardian sent you towards the station while they investigated the other signals. You were supposed to observe, gather information and call for help if the Glitch appeared.

It appeared before you reached the stairs.

The creature had grown larger since the rooftop encounter. Its outline shifted between different shapes, as if several versions of it were trying to occupy the same space. Every time it moved, the lights above the platform flickered. People were rushing towards the exits, confused by the sudden power failure.

Your first instinct was to chase the creature. You wanted to prove that the training had worked. But you heard a child crying near the ticket gates, and you saw an elderly man struggling to move through the crowd. The Glitch was dangerous, but so was the panic spreading around it.

You changed direction. Web lines caught a falling sign before it hit the floor. Another web pulled a loose barrier away from the crowd. You guided people towards the exit, trying to sound calmer than you felt. Your hands shook, but the webs held.

By the time you turned back, the Glitch had reached the train tracks. A wave of distorted energy travelled down the platform. The station lights blinked out, and for a moment you saw another city through the walls: the same station, but empty, flooded with violet light and covered in the blue symbol.

The guardian's voice crackled through your wrist device. “Don't attack its centre. The signal is protecting it. Find the anchor keeping the fracture open.” You looked around and noticed a pulsing crystal shape floating above the tracks.

The Glitch turned towards you. You wanted to run. Instead, you fired two webs at the ceiling beams and swung over the platform. The creature struck at you, but you changed direction at the last second. You landed beside the anchor and wrapped it in web after web until its light began to dim.

The fracture folded in on itself with a sound like glass humming. The Glitch dissolved into harmless sparks. For a moment, the station was silent. Then the lights came back, the trains started moving, and the city continued.

You had protected people without defeating the creature through strength alone. You had noticed what needed doing and made a choice. When the guardian arrived, they looked at the sealed fracture, then at you. “You're learning,” they said.

Your wrist device lit up one last time. A new message appeared beneath the blue symbol: CONNECTION ESTABLISHED. The guardian's expression changed. “That isn't our system,” they whispered. “Something just noticed you.”

CHAPTER SIX

THE HERO YOU CHOOSE TO BE

Morning arrived with a pale pink glow over the rooftops. The city looked ordinary again, but you knew better than to trust appearances. You sat on the edge of a building with the guardian, watching the first trains cross the bridges. Your suit was scuffed, your arms ached from swinging and your phone had finally given up and died.

The guardian explained that closing one fracture had only stopped one opening. The message on your device meant the source had recognised the connection between you and the signal. It might be looking for you. It might even be trying to communicate. Nobody knew whether that was a warning or an invitation.

You thought about the night before: the frightened people at the station, the falling sign, the moment you had nearly chased the Glitch instead of helping those who needed you. You had not done everything perfectly. You had been scared, made mistakes and needed help. But you had stayed long enough to make a difference.

“What happens now?” you asked. The guardian looked across the skyline. “Now you decide what kind of hero you want to be. You can keep learning. You can investigate the signal. You can help protect this city. But nobody else gets to make that choice for you.”

You stood and tested the web-shooters. A blue thread stretched from your wrist to a rooftop antenna, steady and bright. Below, the streets were filling with people heading to school, work and places they had promised to be. Most of them would never know what had happened beneath the station the night before.

Maybe that was part of the job. A hero did not always get applause. Sometimes the reward was simply seeing people get home safely. You pulled your mask into place and looked towards the tallest building in the city. Somewhere beyond it, another pulse answered the one inside your wrist device.

You took a running step, fired a web and leapt into the morning. The first swing was uneven. The second was better. By the third, you were moving above the streets with the wind rushing past your mask and the whole city opening beneath you.

You still did not know who had sent the signal. You did not know how many universes were connected to it, or what waited beyond the next fracture. But you were no longer just a witness to the mystery. You were part of the story now, and you had a say in how it would end.
